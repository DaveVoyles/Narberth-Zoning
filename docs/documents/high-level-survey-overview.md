# High-Level Overview of Raw Survey Results

## Purpose of this overview

This document provides a high-level orientation to the survey results in `Raw Survey Results.pdf`. It is intended as a starting point for later, more thorough analysis. It does not attempt to evaluate the responses, measure sentiment, quantify themes, or draw conclusions.

## What the source document is

The PDF is a raw export of a survey titled **"Survey on Housing Affordability in Narberth."** It appears to contain both summary-style survey output and individual written responses from respondents.

The opening pages establish the general context:

- The survey asks whether respondents live in Narberth Borough.
- It asks respondents what they think about housing affordability in Narberth.
- Early written responses show that respondents discuss housing prices, rental costs, home values, redevelopment, density, parking, traffic, Narberth's small-town character, and whether borough policy should address affordability.

The document is not a narrative report. It is primarily a collection of survey questions and raw responses, organized by question and page.

## Overall subject matter

The survey centers on housing affordability and proposed zoning changes in Narberth Borough. Respondents comment on how housing costs have changed, what kinds of housing should be encouraged or limited, and how proposed zoning updates might affect the borough.

Across the sections reviewed, responses commonly refer to:

- Housing affordability and whether Narberth has an affordability problem.
- New apartments, mixed-use buildings, rowhouses, cottages, accessory dwelling units, and other housing types.
- Building height, density, setbacks, impervious coverage, and neighborhood scale.
- Parking requirements, street parking, off-site parking, and assumptions about car ownership.
- Traffic, pedestrian safety, public transit, SEPTA access, and walkability.
- Narberth's character, downtown vitality, historic feel, green space, and quality of life.
- The role of developers, borough government, the Planning Commission, and existing zoning rules.

## Pages 77-89: Zone 5B / Montgomery Avenue

Pages 77 through 89 focus on the question:

> What do you think about Narberth Planning Commission's proposed zoning changes in the 5B zoning district (Montgomery Avenue)?

This section contains individual written responses to proposed zoning changes for the 5B district along Montgomery Avenue. The page header indicates that 303 respondents answered this question and 140 skipped it.

At a high level, the responses discuss proposed changes related to development along Montgomery Avenue. The comments refer to building height, additional residential units, mixed-use development, affordable housing incentives, reduced parking requirements, and the relationship between Montgomery Avenue and the rest of Narberth.

Some respondents describe Montgomery Avenue as a more appropriate location for additional density or redevelopment than other parts of the borough. Others express concern that taller buildings, more apartments, or reduced parking would affect nearby residential streets, traffic, pedestrian safety, and the small-town feel of Narberth.

Parking is a frequent topic in this section. Respondents discuss whether reduced parking requirements are realistic, whether residents would still need cars, how overflow parking might affect side streets, and whether SEPTA or bus access can substitute for on-site parking.

The section also includes comments about whether development along Montgomery Avenue could support local businesses, improve an underused corridor, or provide more housing options. Other responses question whether the proposed changes would actually create affordability or primarily benefit developers.

## Pages 90-105: Zone 4A

Pages 90 through the end of the document focus on the question:

> What do you think about Narberth Planning Commission's proposed zoning changes in the 4A zoning district?

This section also shows 303 respondents answering and 140 skipping the question. It contains individual written responses about proposed zoning changes in the 4A district.

At a high level, respondents discuss possible changes to housing types, density, apartment buildings, rowhouses, cottages, accessory dwelling units, height limits, parking requirements, setbacks, and lot coverage. The responses often place these changes in the context of downtown Narberth, nearby residential streets, and areas close to the train station.

Many comments focus on how 4A differs from Montgomery Avenue. Some respondents appear more cautious about applying greater density or taller buildings in 4A, especially where the district is closer to existing residential blocks, historic homes, narrow streets, or downtown areas. Others support additional housing options near transit or downtown businesses.

As with the 5B section, parking is a major subject. Respondents comment on the proposed reduction in parking requirements, street parking pressure, visitor parking, off-site parking, household car ownership, and whether SEPTA access should affect parking rules.

The section also includes comments about neighborhood character, building scale, green space, impervious surface, traffic, school and infrastructure capacity, and whether zoning changes should be made before existing development issues or vacant storefronts are addressed.
